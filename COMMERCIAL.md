# Commercial licensing

CFPress is **dual-licensed**. You may use it under the **AGPL-3.0** (see [LICENSE](LICENSE)),
or under a **commercial license** obtained from the copyright holder. You do not need both —
you choose whichever one fits your use.

This page explains which one you need. It is a plain-language summary; the license texts
themselves are what actually govern.

## You do NOT need a commercial license if

- You run CFPress for your own site, your organisation's sites, or internal systems.
- You evaluate it, study it, modify it privately, or use it in research.
- You ship it as part of a project that is itself licensed under the AGPL-3.0 or a compatible license.
- You run an **unmodified** copy as a network service. The AGPL's network clause applies to
  *modified* versions; if you have not changed the source, there is nothing extra to offer.

## You DO need a commercial license if

- You offer CFPress to third parties as a **hosted or managed service**, and you do not want to
  release your modifications under the AGPL.
- You **embed CFPress in a closed-source product** that you distribute.
- Your organisation's policy **prohibits AGPL-licensed software**, and you still want to use CFPress.

## What the commercial license gives you

- The right to use, modify and distribute CFPress **without** the AGPL's source-disclosure obligations.
- The right to offer it as a hosted service **without** publishing your changes.
- A written grant your legal team can file.

## How to obtain one

Contact **`licensing@<your-domain>`** with a short description of your intended use. Pricing is
sized to the use — a small managed service and a large platform are not the same license, and
the goal is not to make the small ones unaffordable.

## Why the project can offer this at all

Dual licensing requires that the copyright holder owns — or holds sufficient rights to — **all**
of the code being licensed. That is why contributions require a [CLA](CLA.md).

Without a CLA, a single merged pull request would permanently remove the project's ability to
offer a commercial option, because that contributor's code could not be relicensed. The CLA is
not about taking contributors' rights away; it is what keeps both licensing paths open.
